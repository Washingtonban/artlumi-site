01 / MEET ARTLUMI

A project born
in spare time.

During the week, Kellen and Washington work in tech at large companies. On weekends, the living room table becomes a workbench. Ideas move from notebook to sketch to prototype, then, after plenty of testing, to something you can hold.

Artlumi brings those two worlds together. Technology brings the method: measure, test, learn, and improve. Creativity brings color, warmth, and a wish to make everyday life a little lovelier and easier.

Made for people who create in their spare time.

Artlumi is for people like us: creative people who spend the week at work and save their free time to invent, draw, build and decorate. Whatever the theme, be it a new shelf, a handmade gift or a coloring book to share with the kids, if you have an idea waiting for the weekend, you're in the right place.

How an idea becomes Artlumi

  1. 1. Idea

    It starts with a real household problem or a chat with the children.

  2. 2. Design

    We model it on a computer, carefully planning every measurement and fit.

  3. 3. Prototype

    We print, test, and adjust until everything feels just right.

  4. 4. For you

    It only becomes a product after we have used it ourselves.

03 / THE PEOPLE BEHIND IT

Two people,
many ideas.

She's a machine learning engineer; he's a software architect. Engineers by training and makers at heart, Kellen and Washington came together around a simple idea: create things that solve life's little problems.

Kellen Guimarães

Machine learning engineer

Born on the banks of Rio Cachoeri near Oriximiná, Pará, Kellen learned to read at three. That early love of words still inspires her children's books. A mechatronics engineer with a master's in electrical engineering, she studied at CSUN in California and now works in machine learning. She loves drawing and painting; at Artlumi, she asks what a product should do, for whom, and why.

Instagram ↗︎LinkedIn ↗︎

Washington Barbosa

Software architect and maker

Born in Goiana, Pernambuco, Washington always dreamed of becoming an engineer. Watching Iron Man led him to mechatronics: he wanted to build his own suit. The suit is still a work in progress, but the workshop is real. Today he's a software architect who spends weekends on 3D modeling, printing, and mechanical keyboards. At Artlumi, he turns sketches into prototypes and prototypes into products.
